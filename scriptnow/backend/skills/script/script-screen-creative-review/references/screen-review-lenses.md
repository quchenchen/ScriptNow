# Screen review lenses

These lenses turn a user-selected review focus into a bounded review task. They are not score
templates and do not replace evidence from the work.

## Shared evidence protocol

For every material finding:

1. Name the scene, episode, beat, page, or quoted passage that supports it.
2. Mark the statement as **fact**, **inference**, or **recommendation**.
3. Explain the dramatic or production consequence.
4. Offer the smallest useful next action without silently rewriting the work.
5. State what cannot be concluded when the available evidence is incomplete.

Do not convert market conventions, episode ratios, production assumptions, or numeric scores into
blocking rules unless the review brief explicitly selects that rubric.

## `overall` — 综合评审

Establish the work's intended audience, format, story promise, and requested decision. Summarize the
highest-impact findings across structure, character, pacing, audience, and production. Prefer a few
well-supported priorities over an exhaustive checklist.

## `structure` — 故事结构

Trace premise delivery, causal turns, escalation, reversals, climax, and payoff. Check whether each
major turn is earned by earlier action and whether promises are paid off. Do not require a preferred
act model unless the work or brief selected one.

## `character` — 人物关系

Examine agency, desire, opposition, choice, consequence, relationship turns, and voice. Distinguish
what a character does from what exposition says about them. Treat consent, coercion, and power as
dramatic evidence rather than automatic genre prohibitions.

## `pacing` — 节奏与钩子

Check scene function, information flow, escalation, repetition, entry and exit points, episode
momentum, and emotional breathing room. A hook must grow from an unresolved dramatic question; do
not require a cliffhanger at every boundary or apply a serial rule to a complete short work.

## `market` — 受众与市场

Evaluate audience promise, genre legibility, format fit, differentiation, and likely production or
distribution constraints. Label audience and market claims as hypotheses unless the run includes
current source data. Never present popularity assumptions as facts about the work.

## `adaptation` — 改编潜力

Separate what must be preserved from what may change. Examine visualizability, scene concentration,
external action, recurring locations, cast load, props, effects, episodic expansion or compression,
and the cost of translating narration into screen behavior. Keep prose quality and adaptation
potential as distinct judgments.
