# Pacing and rhythm check

## Before drafting

1. **Count story beats for this chapter.** A chapter should contain at most TWO major narrative breakthroughs (revelation, alliance formation, betrayal, death, power shift). More than two means the chapter is overloaded — split it.

2. **Identify the chapter's dramatic engine.** Write in one sentence what the chapter MUST accomplish. Everything else is optional. If you cannot articulate the single irreducible task, the chapter lacks focus.

3. **Check the hook position.** The first 300 words must contain one of: a sensory detail that raises a question, a character in motion with something at stake, or a decision being made under pressure. Do NOT open with environmental description, character reflection, or world-building exposition.

4. **Check the ending propulsion.** The final paragraph must change the situation in a way that makes the reader WANT the next chapter. This does NOT mean a cliffhanger — it means a state change that creates a new question, raises the cost of inaction, or reveals a consequence the character must address.

## After drafting

5. **Measure information density.** Count significant plot turns. If a chapter contains more than 3 weight-class events (defined as: changes to character goals, faction alignments, relationship states, knowledge boundaries, or life/death status), flag it for splitting.

6. **Check for transitional signals.** Between major revelations, there MUST be at least one passage where a character reflects on what was just learned and reorients their understanding. Without these breather moments, revelations blur together.

7. **Verify chapter length against scene count.** A chapter under 3000 words should not attempt more than 1 major turn. A chapter between 4000-7000 words can handle 2 turns with proper spacing. Chapters over 7000 words should distribute turns across clearly distinguished scenes.

## Output
After completing these checks, either confirm "Pacing acceptable" or list specific issues with location and remedy.
