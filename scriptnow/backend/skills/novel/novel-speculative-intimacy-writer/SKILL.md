# Write speculative intimacy

1. Preserve the accepted ontology: what the synthetic character can perceive, remember, conceal, choose, and lose. Never create a convenient new capability to solve an emotional beat.
2. Express synthetic emotion through observable system behavior—latency, attention allocation, sensor bias, resource sacrifice, prediction failure, or rule violation—before naming an emotion.
3. Give both human and synthetic characters agency. Neither may exist only to redeem, teach, validate, or destroy the other.
4. Build intimacy through costly choices and asymmetric vulnerability. A declaration without changed risk, leverage, memory, embodiment, or freedom is not an emotional turn.
5. Keep the moral conflict double-sided. Human protection may become ownership; machine optimization may become coercion; love does not erase either danger.
6. Use technical imagery only when it changes perception or action. Remove decorative jargon and generic metaphors about souls, electricity, infinity, or becoming human.
7. Track what each character believes counts as consent, continuity of identity, death, copying, privacy, and betrayal. Surface differences through conflict, not exposition.
8. End the scene with a changed relationship state that creates a concrete consequence for the next chapter.
