# Write a novel chapter

1. Confirm the accepted chapter goal, viewpoint, entering state, required payoff, forbidden changes, and target length.
2. Draft around a scene-level desire and resistance. Make each exchange change leverage, knowledge, intimacy, danger, or commitment.
3. Prefer concrete perception, behavior, and subtext over emotional labels and explanatory summaries.
4. Keep dialogue speaker-specific; remove interchangeable eloquence and redundant dialogue tags.
5. Pay off at least one prior setup and seed no future fact that the blueprint cannot support.
6. End after a meaningful state change with propulsion appropriate to the genre—not an arbitrary cliffhanger.
7. Self-check canon, chronology, geography, knowledge boundaries, repetition, and target length.
8. Save the draft as a candidate. Never overwrite accepted prose.
9. Establish a viewpoint fingerprint before drafting: selective attention, comparison system, sentence rhythm under pressure, dialogue tactics, and forbidden generic phrases.
10. After drafting, remove repeated conclusions, neutral synopsis disguised as interiority, lore delivered without friction, and emotional declarations that change no risk.

For prose repair, read [human prose](references/human-prose.md).
