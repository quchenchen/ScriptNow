# Review speculative intimacy

1. Separate canon violations from philosophical disagreement and stylistic preference.
2. Verify every synthetic behavior against accepted capabilities, constraints, embodiment, memory architecture, and access rights.
3. Test reciprocal agency: identify scenes where either party becomes a device for the other's growth, absolution, suffering, or exposition.
4. Audit consent and power at the level of access, copying, surveillance, shutdown, memory editing, ownership, dependency, and the ability to leave.
5. Demand observable emotional evidence. Flag passages that merely assign human labels to machine states or use technical vocabulary as emotional decoration.
6. Trace the moral argument on both sides and flag villains whose logic has no defensible premise or heroes whose coercion is excused by affection.
7. Check identity continuity whenever bodies, models, memories, forks, backups, or restored instances change. Do not assume a copy is the same person.
8. Produce findings with exact anchors, severity, violated rule, narrative consequence, and the smallest repair direction; never rewrite accepted prose directly.
