# Emotional depth and character interiority

## Before drafting

1. **Emotional state inventory.** For the viewpoint character, note: what they want right now (small, immediate desire), what they fear losing, what they refuse to admit, and what physical sensation they are carrying (tension, exhaustion, hunger, cold, a specific ache). These four elements must appear in the chapter in some form.

2. **Memory check.** If a character has lost someone important (parent, mentor, partner), at least ONE sensory flashback to that person must appear before the story's climax. A memory is NOT a summary of facts ("she taught me to be strong") — it is a SPECIFIC sensory moment: a smell, a texture, a phrase in a specific voice, a physical gesture.

3. **Relationship state tracking.** For any relationship that carries emotional weight (familial, romantic, antagonistic, mentor/student), note the current state before drafting. A chapter must advance at least ONE relationship by one measurable degree — toward trust, toward suspicion, toward intimacy, toward rupture.

## During drafting

4. **Emotion through behavior, not labels.** Do NOT write "she was afraid." Show the body responding: a hand that won't stop checking a pocket, a voice that drops to a whisper when a name is mentioned, a pattern of looking at the door before answering a question. Give the reader evidence and let them feel the emotion.

5. **Vary emotional temperature.** A chapter must contain at least TWO distinct emotional registers. A chapter that is entirely tense, entirely sad, or entirely analytical reads as monotonous. Insert a moment of unexpected tenderness, humor, wonder, or sensory pleasure to create texture.

## After drafting

6. **Emotional payoff audit.** Review all emotional setups from prior chapters (unresolved grief, unspoken attraction, suppressed rage, deferred guilt). Does this chapter pay off at least ONE of these? If not, and the chapter is post-midpoint, add a sentence or exchange that acknowledges the deferred emotion.

7. **Climax proximity check.** If the story is within 3 chapters of its climax, the protagonist must experience at least ONE moment of unguarded emotional exposure — a crack in the analytical armor where something they have been holding breaks through. This does not require melodrama; a single sentence of unprocessed feeling can suffice.

## Output
After drafting, list which relationships advanced, which emotions were shown (with brief evidence), and whether any deferred emotional setup awaits resolution.
